Aeroplanes are designed to withstand lightning strikes, can fly for hours on one engine, and have doors that cannot be opened mid-flight due to pressure differences. The "black box" flight recorder is actually orange to make it easier to find in a crash, and the fastest aircraft ever built was the Lockheed SR-71 Blackbird. Other facts include pilots not eating the same meals to prevent food poisoning and planes being built with lightweight materials like aluminum and carbon fiber for fuel efficiency.

Safety and design:
  • Lightning: Aeroplanes are frequently hit by lightning, but the strikes typically pass through the plane's exterior without causing damage.
  • One engine: Most commercial jets can fly for hours on one engine, which is part of the redundancy built into their design for safety.
  • Doors: Airplane doors cannot be opened in mid-flight because the pressure difference between the pressurized cabin and the lower external air pressure makes it physically impossible.
  • "Black box": Despite the name, the flight recorder is painted bright orange to be more visible in case of an accident.
  • Windows: Airplane windows have a small hole in the middle layer to equalize pressure and are rounded to distribute air pressure evenly.
  • Lights: The wings of a plane have red lights on the left and green lights on the right to help other pilots determine its direction at night.
